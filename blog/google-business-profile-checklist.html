Google Business Profile: A Practical Checklist for Small Businesses

Published November 12, 2026 · Bizgrowly

When someone nearby searches for a service like yours, one of the first things they often see is not your website but your Google Business Profile: the panel with your name, hours, photos, reviews and a button to call or get directions. For many small businesses it quietly works as a second homepage. The good news is that it's free, and most of what makes it useful comes down to steady, simple habits. This checklist walks you through setting it up properly and keeping it in good shape.

1. Claim and verify the profile

Start by searching for your business name on Google and Google Maps. If a profile already exists, claim it rather than creating a duplicate. If none exists, create one at Google's Business Profile page using an account you'll keep long-term, ideally a business account rather than a former employee's personal email.

Google will ask you to verify that you own the business. The method varies (it may be a video, a phone call, a postcard or another option), so follow whatever your profile offers. Until verification is complete, many edits won't show publicly, so do this first.

2. Get the basics exactly right

The core details matter more than any clever extra. Check each one carefully:

  • Business name. Use your real-world name as it appears on your sign and documents. Adding keywords or city names to the name goes against Google's guidelines and can get a profile suspended.
  • Primary category. Choose the most specific category that describes what you mainly do, then add a few secondary categories only if they truly apply.
  • Address or service area. If customers visit you, show your address. If you travel to customers, set a service area and hide the address if you don't serve people there.
  • Phone and website. Use a number that is actually answered during your listed hours, and link to the page that best matches what local customers want, not always the homepage.
  • Hours. Keep regular hours accurate and add special hours for holidays. Few things lose a customer faster than showing up to a closed door.

Make sure your name, address and phone number match what's on your website and other listings. Consistency helps both people and search engines trust that the information is current.

3. Write a description that helps people decide

Your description should answer the questions a new customer has: what you do, who you serve, where you work and what makes you a good choice. Write it for people first. Mention your main services naturally, skip the hype, and leave out links and promotional phrases like "best in town," which add nothing. A few clear sentences beat a wall of keywords.

4. Add services or products

Depending on your category, you can list services or products with short descriptions and, if you wish, prices. This lets people see what you offer without leaving Google, and it gives you room to describe specialties that don't fit in the main description. Keep the list tidy and remove anything you no longer offer.

5. Use real photos, and keep adding them

Photos are often what convinces someone to call or visit. Upload a clear logo and cover image, then add real pictures of your storefront (so people can recognize it from the street), your interior, your team at work and your finished products or projects. Avoid stock images; customers can tell, and they want to see the real place. Adding a few new photos each month signals that the business is active.

6. Make reviews part of your routine

Reviews are one of the most visible parts of your profile. Rather than asking everyone at once, build a simple habit:

  • Ask satisfied customers for a review at a natural moment, such as right after a job is finished or a purchase is picked up.
  • Make it easy by sharing the direct review link from your profile in a text, an email or a small card at the counter.
  • Never offer discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews, and don't write reviews for yourself. Both break Google's policies.
  • Reply to every review, positive or negative. Thank people by name, and respond to criticism calmly, briefly and with an offer to make things right offline.

Future customers read your replies as much as the reviews themselves. A thoughtful answer to a complaint can build more trust than a dozen five-star ratings.

7. Post updates and answer questions

You can publish short posts on your profile about offers, events, new products or seasonal news. They don't need to be elaborate; a photo and two sentences are enough. Posting regularly keeps the profile fresh and gives people a reason to choose you today.

Also keep an eye on questions customers ask, and on edits Google or the public suggest to your information. Answer questions promptly and review suggested edits so nobody changes your hours or phone number without you noticing.

8. Connect the profile to your website and measure results

Your profile and your website should work as a team. When people click through, they should land on a fast, mobile-friendly page that repeats the key details and makes it easy to call, book or buy. Adding a UTM tag to your profile's website link lets you see those visits separately in Google Analytics, so you know how many people arrive from your profile and what they do next.

Inside the profile itself, check the performance figures Google provides, such as how people found you and how many calls, direction requests and website clicks you received. Look at the trend month to month rather than reacting to a single week.

A simple monthly routine

Once the profile is set up, about half an hour a month keeps it healthy:

  • Confirm hours, phone and services are still correct, and add special hours for upcoming holidays.
  • Upload a few new photos.
  • Publish one or two short posts.
  • Reply to any new reviews and questions.
  • Note your calls, direction requests and website clicks, and compare them with last month.
